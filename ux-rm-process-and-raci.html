DISCUSSION DRAFT · REVISION 6 · SHARED OPERATIONAL OWNERSHIP · SEPARATE FUNDING AUTHORITY

UX and Requirements Management

Tradecraft and RM share operational ownership through distinct decisions. UX leads user research and experience design; the development department controls funding, contracts, and implementation.

Proposed agreement: Tradecraft confirms operational suitability. RM confirms operational requirements satisfaction. Both confirmations are required for operational acceptance. The development program owner separately authorizes funded delivery and release. UX retains research, design, and usability-validation authority.

Purpose and organizational roles

This proposed agreement reflects shared operational ownership by Tradecraft and RM, with funding and contracts controlled by the development department. It uses five columns: Development program / funding owner, Tradecraft owner, UX, RM, and Engineering.

The development program / funding owner is the named person authorized to approve investment, contracts, funded scope, delivery commitments, and release authorization. Engineering includes development, QA, and testing within that department. Department membership does not transfer decision rights between these two roles.

Tradecraft supplies SMEs and owns operational success expectations and tradecraft correctness. RM consolidates operational needs into a coherent requirements scope, coordinates operational priorities, and owns requirements acceptance. Both participate in operational ownership through separate, explicit decisions.

User communications and support-related work remain activities assigned to UX and RM. No separate communications or control-authority team is introduced. Existing organizational approvals still apply; this agreement grants no new waiver authority.

Decision boundaries

Tradecraft owns the meaning of tradecraft rules, intended operational outcomes, and confirmation of operational suitability. SMEs advise and review on the owner’s behalf; approval delegation must be explicit. If several tradecraft owners are affected, RM records the named owner and applicable suitability decision for each affected scope.

RM owns requirements quality, traceability, consolidation of operational needs, requirements readiness, operational backlog ordering, and confirmation that agreed operational requirements are satisfied. RM uses Tradecraft, UX, and Engineering evidence; it does not substitute its own opinion for their findings.

UX owns research methods, direct engagement with users, research synthesis, interaction design, prototypes, usability validation, and user-facing story and acceptance-criteria content. Tradecraft and RM define the operational need and constraints collaboratively; they do not prescribe UX methods or overwrite usability findings.

The development program / funding owner approves funding, contracts, funded scope, and delivery authorization. Engineering owns technical design, estimates, feasible delivery plans, implementation, testing, verification, and defect triage. Operational priority does not itself authorize expenditure or commit Engineering capacity.

UX owns user-facing communication content, feedback responses, release notes, and guidance. RM coordinates publication, distribution, change/status records, and follow-up tracking. Engineering confirms technical accuracy and shipped scope.

Process map

Read top to bottom for a delivery slice. This is an iterative loop: new evidence can send work back to discovery, design, or refinement.

1

Define operational success and funding boundaries

Tradecraft and RM frame the need; the program owner sets funding boundaries.

Tradecraft defines intended operational success; RM consolidates needs; UX contributes user evidence. Name decision owners and delegates. The program owner confirms funding and contract boundaries.

2

Discover user needs and tradecraft expectations

UX leads research; Tradecraft supplies SMEs; RM integrates evidence.

UX investigates needs directly with users and synthesizes feedback. Tradecraft confirms operational rules. RM links evidence to requirements; Engineering assesses technical constraints.

3

Explore and validate solutions

UX leads design and user validation.

Tradecraft and RM contribute operational context and constraints; Engineering evaluates feasibility. UX tests alternatives and records usability findings. Iterate as evidence changes.

4

Refine and order operational requirements

RM coordinates; UX owns user-facing content; Tradecraft confirms intent.

UX refines stories; Tradecraft resolves operational-rule questions; Engineering estimates and checks testability. RM reviews quality and orders the operational backlog with Tradecraft and UX input.

5

Agree funded scope and plan the sprint

The program owner authorizes commitments; Engineering plans feasible work.

RM proposes operational priorities and sprint outcomes with Tradecraft and UX. Engineering selects feasible work within funded scope. Changes to funding, contracts, or delivery commitments require program approval.

6

Build, verify, and prepare communications

Engineering builds and tests; UX evaluates; RM tracks readiness.

Engineering records verification evidence; UX evaluates usability and drafts guidance; Tradecraft SMEs assess suitability. RM tracks requirements coverage and coordinates release-note preparation.

7

Confirm operational acceptance, authorize release, and learn

Tradecraft and RM confirm separately; the program owner authorizes release.

Tradecraft confirms suitability; RM confirms requirements satisfaction. Both are required. After program authorization, RM publishes UX-owned communications. UX measures outcomes, Tradecraft assesses operational improvement, and RM prioritizes corrective action within funded boundaries.

Discovery and feedback ↔ Design and testing ↔ Backlog refinement → Sprint planning → Delivery and release communications → Outcome learning ↺

Tradecraft and RM share operational ownership through separate accountable activities. UX and Engineering retain their discipline decisions. Operational acceptance requires both Tradecraft and RM confirmations; funding and release authorization remain with the development program owner.

Shared operational ownership and two acceptance decisions

  • Tradecraft confirmation: the capability supports the intended operational work and meets agreed tradecraft expectations. Tradecraft reviews SME input, UX evidence, and implementation evidence and records its suitability decision.
  • RM confirmation: the agreed operational requirements are covered, evidence is complete enough for acceptance, and unresolved gaps have an authorized disposition. RM records this decision separately from the Tradecraft suitability decision.
  • Both confirmations are required for operational acceptance of the affected scope. Neither confirmation substitutes for the other. UX supplies its own usability assessment, and Engineering supplies verification results; acceptance does not relabel an untested assumption or failed test as validated.
  • The development program / funding owner separately authorizes delivery and release after both confirmations and other required approvals. Funding approval alone is not operational acceptance. If operationally acceptable scope cannot be funded, escalate the tradeoff rather than silently changing the acceptance standard.
  • For every acceptance decision, record scope/version, criteria, evidence, decision maker, unresolved gaps, permitted risk decisions, and date. A reduced delivery slice requires revised criteria and both operational confirmations for that slice.

Named authority, disputes, and escalation

  • Before adoption, name the program/funding owner, Tradecraft owner or owners, RM lead, UX lead, Engineering lead, delegates, authority limits, response windows, and a common executive sponsor. RM maintains the register. Silence or meeting facilitation does not transfer authority.
  • Tradecraft resolves tradecraft meaning and suitability. RM resolves requirements integration, operational ordering, and requirements acceptance. UX resolves research and experience-design decisions. Engineering resolves technical design and feasibility. The program owner resolves funding, contract, and delivery-commitment decisions.
  • If Tradecraft and RM disagree on operational acceptance, the affected scope remains unaccepted. Escalate to their named common sponsor for resolution of scope or expectations, then record both decisions. If the dispute concerns affordability, involve the program owner. A sponsor decision does not fabricate evidence or waive obligations beyond that sponsor’s authority.
  • RM coordinates any approvals required under existing organizational policy with technical support from Engineering and user-impact evidence from UX. The person with actual delegated authority makes an exception decision; no RACI assignment creates new authority.

Validation, requirements review, and readiness

  • UX validates needs and solutions with users. Engineering verifies implementation. RM reviews requirement quality and coordinates readiness. Tradecraft confirms operational suitability. These are distinct activities with separate evidence.
  • RM agrees requirements-readiness criteria with Tradecraft, UX, and Engineering; the program owner is consulted on delivery implications. Engineering separately confirms feasibility and capacity. RM review completion does not approve another team’s design or change user-story intent.
  • Any team can flag an evidenced blocker: an unresolved mandatory constraint, failed critical criterion, uncertainty preventing meaningful implementation or verification, or material feasibility conflict. RM records the finding, affected scope, owner, and due date. The appropriate owner resolves it; only permitted, authorized risk acceptance can dispose of an unresolved gap.
  • Use evidence proportionate to the delivery slice. Template preferences do not create new gates. UX and Engineering correct their own content; Tradecraft resolves operational-rule ambiguity; RM coordinates consistency and traceability.

Story changes and nonfunctional requirements

  • UX approves within-scope user-facing behavior and acceptance-criteria changes with Engineering consultation. RM records meaningful changes. Tradecraft is consulted where operational practice or suitability is affected. Editorial corrections need no new approval cycle.
  • RM consolidates changes to operational scope and priorities with Tradecraft and UX. Changes affecting tradecraft suitability require Tradecraft confirmation; funding, contracts, or delivery commitments require program-owner approval and a feasible Engineering plan. RM cannot unilaterally add funded work.
  • UX defines accessible interactions. Engineering defines and implements technical security, privacy, accessibility, performance, reliability, and operational requirements and verifies them. RM establishes traceable coverage of applicable requirements and coordinates required interpretations and approvals. Technical implementation remains Engineering’s responsibility.
  • Each applicable requirement has a source, measurable threshold, measurement conditions, owner, and evidence. Policy exceptions follow existing authority limits, regardless of which department documents the requirement.

User feedback and communication ownership

  • UX owns feedback intake, acknowledgment, direct user contact, research synthesis, and responses. RM helps log, route, and track feedback and links it to requirements and backlog items. Tradecraft SMEs add operational context without becoming a gate on direct user research.
  • Engineering triages suspected defects; UX investigates usability and unmet needs; Tradecraft interprets operational impact; RM integrates and orders the resulting work. Funding or commitment impacts go to the program owner. Feature requests are evidence to investigate, not automatic commitments.
  • UX closes the loop with users using accurate disposition and timing records maintained by RM. Neither team promises unapproved features or dates. Urgent incidents follow the existing incident process.

Backlog refinement and sprint planning

  • RM facilitates refinement (grooming), integrates operational needs, orders the operational backlog using Tradecraft and UX input, and records unresolved questions. UX refines user stories; Engineering contributes splitting, estimates, dependencies, and testability. Tradecraft clarifies intended practice.
  • Engineering facilitates sprint planning. RM agrees operational priorities and intended sprint outcomes with Tradecraft and UX; Engineering selects feasible work and plans execution within approved capacity and contracted scope. The program owner approves changes to funding or delivery commitments.
  • Operational backlog priority and funded delivery selection are separate decisions. RM cannot commit spending or capacity, and the program owner cannot declare an unsuitable solution operationally accepted. Record the negotiated delivery slice and any deferred needs.
  • UX research and design can run ahead of or alongside development. Refinement and sprint ceremonies do not place UX methods under RM or Engineering. Mid-sprint changes require impact assessment, operational agreement, and program approval when commitments change.

Defects, risk, and release

  • Engineering coordinates evidence-based defect severity, fixes, verification, and retesting; UX re-evaluates experience issues and Tradecraft assesses operational implications. RM prioritizes operational remediation within constraints. The program owner approves additional funding or changed commitments.
  • Tradecraft accepts only residual tradecraft suitability risk within its delegated authority. RM accepts only residual requirements and usability gaps within its delegated operational authority, with UX advice and Tradecraft consultation. Cross-cutting gaps require both affected operational decisions. The program owner decides budget and schedule exposure, not the truth of user or operational evidence.
  • RM records each permitted risk acceptance with scope, evidence, impact, accepting person and authority, rationale, mitigation, remediation owner/date, and review trigger. Nonwaivable obligations remain blockers; required external approvals use existing organizational processes.
  • Release requires Tradecraft’s suitability confirmation, RM’s requirements confirmation, Engineering verification evidence, UX findings, and other required approvals, followed by program-owner release authorization. Prioritizing an issue differently does not erase its severity or failed evidence.

Release notes and user communications

  • UX is accountable for clear release notes and guidance. RM assembles change records and coordinates preparation and distribution. Engineering confirms actual delivered scope, fixes, rollout limits, and known issues; Tradecraft is consulted on operational impacts.
  • UX owns final user-facing wording and actions. RM publishes through agreed channels at authorized timing. The program owner authorizes release announcement timing and external delivery commitments after operational acceptance; RM coordinates that decision.
  • UX maintains help, FAQs, workflow guidance, and routine responses; RM maintains accurate change/status references and tracks updates. Engineering verifies technical instructions. Routine factual replies within approved guidance do not require per-message approval.
  • For each communication, record audience, UX content owner, RM publication owner, channel, and due date. RM coordinates broad operational announcements with Tradecraft input; UX retains research recruitment and research follow-up ownership.

Outcome measurement and corrective action

  • Tradecraft defines operational success expectations. UX develops measurable user outcomes and evaluation methods; RM agrees the integrated targets, review dates, and corrective triggers with Tradecraft and UX. Engineering provides instrumentation and operational monitoring.
  • UX reports outcome evidence; Tradecraft assesses operational suitability; RM prioritizes and assigns corrective actions or records a justified time-bound deferral. The program owner authorizes any funding or commitment changes. RM tracks the actions against the original needs.
  • UX and Engineering make their respective changes; Engineering verifies implementation; UX checks user improvement; Tradecraft confirms operational improvement. RM closes requirements corrective actions on evidence or permitted risk acceptance. Where suitability is affected, Tradecraft’s confirmation is also required.

Working cadence and shared records

  • Keep one shared record linking user evidence, tradecraft expectations, operational scope, stories, funded commitments, tests, decisions, both acceptance confirmations, communications, and corrective actions. RM maintains traceability; each team owns its source content.
  • Collaborate early, refine delivery slices together, remain available during implementation, and review post-release outcomes. Pilot the agreement and assess decision delays, rework, late defects, and operational and user outcomes.
