Requirements Development & Validation Operating Model

Merged operating agreement for the User Experience team, the Requirements Management team, and partner functions. Combines the Rev A boundary model with the operational scaffolding of the Rev 3 counter-draft.

Section 1Operating principle

RM ensures the organization can prove its requirements are good. UX ensures they actually are. The sponsor accepts the risk of shipping them.

No single person owns the product. The research owns the product, UX is accountable for the research, RM is accountable for the integrity of the record, and a narrowly scoped executive sponsor is accountable for release and risk decisions that no delivery team can lawfully make for the business. Accountability is decomposed, not vacant: every activity below carries exactly one accountable role, and no team acquires another team's authority through a documentation, facilitation, or review step.

Section 2Roles and authorities

Six roles. Each has authority within its domain and consults affected partners. Where an authority is unassigned, leadership names it; no team, RM included, inherits unclaimed authority by default.

User Experience

Owns research methods and synthesis, interaction design, prototyping, usability evaluation, user stories and user-facing acceptance criteria, evidence-based prioritization, and post-release outcome evaluation. Adapts methods and iterates within agreed outcomes and constraints without per-activity approval from any other team.

Does not: accept business or compliance risk, waive constraints, or direct engineering implementation.

Requirements Management

Owns requirements conventions, quality review of form, traceability, status, baselines, change-record coordination, the decision register, and refinement facilitation. Flags missing evidence, conflicts, ambiguity, and gaps against agreed criteria, and proposes corrections with the relevant author.

Does not: rewrite validated needs, rank the backlog, approve content or methods, accept risk, or hold Responsible on any decision row.

Executive Sponsor

One named leader (with a named delegate) holding three authorities only: release authorization after required clearances, acceptance of residual business and usability risk within documented limits, and escalation tie-breaks within a recorded response window. Ratifies the prioritization criteria and their quarterly application.

Does not: order the backlog, direct discovery or design, or own day-to-day priorities. This role is episodic and gate-level, not a standing product owner.

Engineering

Owns technical design, technical acceptance criteria, feasibility, estimation, delivery commitments, sprint planning facilitation, and implementation. A delivery commitment certifies feasibility and capacity only; it does not certify user needs or compliance.

QA / Test

Owns the verification approach, test evidence, defect triage coordination, and evidence-based severity. Passing tests establishes conformance to agreed criteria, not user value.

Domain / control authorities

Named owners, one per domain, for business rules, accessibility, security, privacy, and operational targets. Each approves the correctness and applicability of its own rules and any permitted exceptions. Stakeholders and mission owners state problems into intake; they do not direct solutions. A user comms / support function is assigned to an existing communications or change-management lead; UX and RM do not inherit it.

Section 3The boundary at a glance

RM directs the management process. UX directs the development process. Neither directs the other. The sponsor sits above both for exactly two gates.

UX owns content

  • Evidence-based prioritization of the queuecriteria published in this charter; sponsor ratifies quarterly
  • User research and discoveryincluding authority to reframe or kill a need on evidence
  • Prototyping, usability testing, accessible interaction design
  • User stories and user-facing acceptance criteria
  • Validation with usersare these the right requirements?
  • Within-scope content change approval post-baseline
  • Dev handoff and post-release outcome evaluation

RM owns integrity

  • Need intake, logging, and trace IDsnothing enters the pipeline unsourced
  • Requirement quality reviewform only: unambiguous, testable, singular, feasible, traceable
  • Validation of the setcomplete, consistent, conflict-free
  • Traceability, status, and change history, end to end
  • Baseline and configuration control
  • Change control and refinement facilitationruns the process and the record; approves nothing
  • Decision register, risk-acceptance records, trace closure, audit

Sponsor gates

Release authorization after required clearances, and acceptance of residual business or usability risk within documented limits. Controlled risks (security, privacy, accessibility, operational) require the named domain authority instead; no person can waive an obligation beyond their authority, and release approval is never implied by RM review completion.

Section 4Process map

Nine phases from stated need to measured outcome, read top to bottom for one delivery slice. The flow is an iterative loop: new evidence can return work to discovery, design, or refinement at any point. Owning a phase means directing how the work is done, not approving what another team produces.

P0

Charter & named authorities Sponsor leads

Before dependent commitments, leadership confirms by name: the executive sponsor and delegate with documented limits, each domain authority, the comms / support assignment, and the escalation route with response windows. The teams agree readiness criteria and material-change thresholds. RM maintains the register; each lead confirms their own entry.

Guardrail: silence is not approval, and no response from a named owner does not transfer their authority to RM or any other team.
P1

Need intake RM leads

Stakeholders, mission owners, and the feedback loop surface needs. RM logs each one, assigns a trace ID, and maintains the queue. Stakeholders state problems, never solutions. A feature request is evidence of a possible need, not an automatic requirement or commitment.

P2

Prioritization UX leads

UX orders the queue on research evidence, user impact, risk, and stakeholder input, with engineering consulted on feasibility and dependencies. RM verifies every prioritized item traces to a logged need.

Guardrail: the criteria are published in this charter. A quarterly review shows the sponsor and stakeholders the queue, the criteria, and the evidence behind the ordering; the sponsor ratifies the criteria and their application, not individual line items.
P3

Discovery & constraints UX leads

UX selects methods, conducts research, and synthesizes needs; comms / support routes ongoing feedback, and UX retains direct access to users. Support intake is never a gate on research. Domain authorities confirm applicable rules and measurable targets; RM traces their sources. UX holds explicit authority to reframe or kill a logged need on evidence; RM records the disposition against the trace ID.

P4

Solution definition UX leads

Wireframes, prototypes, iterative usability testing, accessible interactions, and authoring of stories with user-facing acceptance criteria. Engineering assesses feasibility and defines technical criteria; QA assesses verifiability; domain authorities approve their own requirements. RM is consulted narrowly on quality attributes of form. Never on content or priority.

P5

Validation & quality review Split by type

Validation with users (right requirements) is UX-led. Quality review of the set (clear, consistent, complete for the slice, traceable, verifiable) is RM-led; RM logs findings, and each domain owner resolves findings about its own content. Review completion is never blanket product approval.

P6

Baseline, refinement & commitment RM baselines Eng commits

RM baselines the validated slice and facilitates backlog refinement, preparing quality and traceability questions and recording actions. UX refines story content preserving intent; engineering splits, estimates, and commits capacity; QA checks testability. Engineering facilitates sprint planning and selects feasible work against the UX-ordered backlog. Facilitation conveys no authority to rank work, approve methods, or change story intent.

P7

Build, verify & resolve Eng builds QA verifies

Engineering builds; QA verifies against agreed criteria and coordinates evidence-based defect triage; UX evaluates the implemented experience against validated needs and approves within-scope user-facing changes. Remediation is ordered through the same evidence-based prioritization; changing priority never erases severity or failed evidence. Material changes go to the sponsor after impact assessment, which RM coordinates and records.

P8

Release & risk acceptance Sponsor authorizes

Unmet mandatory controls and agreed release-blocking criteria hold the affected scope unless the named authority grants a permitted exception. The sponsor may accept residual business or usability risk within documented limits after UX, engineering, and QA advice; controlled risks require the relevant domain authority. Every acceptance is recorded with issue, scope, evidence, severity, accepting authority, rationale, mitigation, remediation owner and date, and expiry. RM links the record to the release decision and confirms trace closure.

P9

Outcome measurement UX evaluates

Outcome targets, review dates, and corrective triggers are agreed before release; engineering and the service owner instrument them. At the review date, UX presents user-outcome evidence and recommendations; corrective items enter intake and are prioritized on the same published criteria. Closure requires evidence that outcomes improved or a recorded, authorized risk acceptance.

Section 6What each kind of approval means

Four distinct checks. Passing one never implies passing another, and none of them alone authorizes release.

User & solution validation · UX leads

Does evidence show the problem matters and the proposed or implemented experience works for users?

Requirements quality review · RM leads

Are statements clear, consistent, complete for the slice, traceable, and verifiable? Passing this does not prove the solution is useful.

Technical verification · QA leads with Engineering

Does the implementation satisfy the agreed functional and nonfunctional requirements? Passing tests does not establish user value.

Release & risk acceptance · Sponsor decides

Is the outcome acceptable to release given the evidence and remaining risks? This decision cannot waive obligations outside the sponsor's authority.

Section 7Hard boundaries

Six lines drawn explicitly, so no team relitigates them per project.

RM reviews form, not content

RM's quality review can reject a requirement for being ambiguous or untestable. It cannot reject a requirement for being the wrong requirement, and it does not unilaterally rewrite validated needs or substitute a preferred solution.

RM administers; RM does not decide

RM runs change control, refinement, and the registers, and enforces the agreed process. RM holds no Responsible role on any decision row. Coordinating a record is not drafting the decision.

Facilitation and review completion convey no authority

Facilitating a session grants no right to rank the backlog, approve methods, or change story intent. RM review completion is never product approval or release authorization, and attendance at a ceremony places no team's methods under another's direction.

Blocking power is defined and narrow

A finding can hold the affected delivery slice only when it evidences an unresolved mandatory constraint, an unmet agreed critical criterion, uncertainty that prevents implementation or verification, or a material feasibility or dependency conflict. Preferences about templates, wording, or process order never qualify. Any team can raise a potential blocker; RM records it; the relevant domain owner resolves it; holds apply to affected scope only.

The sponsor is a gate, not a product owner

The sponsor's authority is release, bounded risk acceptance, and escalation tie-breaks. It does not extend to backlog ordering, discovery, design, or day-to-day priority. A decision to proceed against UX advice records the unresolved usability risk and the sponsor's rationale; it does not change the research finding.

Unclaimed authority goes up, never sideways

Where a needed authority is unassigned, leadership names it before dependent commitments. Silence is not approval, and no response does not transfer authority to RM or any other team.

Section 8Escalation, records & adoption

  • Decision register. RM maintains a register of named owners, delegates, documented limits, and response windows. Each lead confirms their own entry; delegates act within recorded limits.
  • Escalation path. Disputed authority or blocker classification escalates to the sponsor's delegate, then the sponsor, within the recorded response window. The sponsor can assign authority and resolve business tradeoffs; the sponsor cannot override nonwaivable obligations.
  • Disputed decisions. Every disputed decision records the accountable person, evidence, options, rationale, affected requirements, and deadline.
  • One system of record. Research evidence, design decisions, stories, constraints, tests, and changes link in a single shared system. No competing UX and RM backlogs describing the same work.
  • Cadence. RM joins discovery and design discussions early as a partner; joint refinement when a slice takes shape; an active clarification channel during delivery; outcome review after release. No standing approval meetings by default.
  • Pilot before formalizing. This agreement runs on one delivery slice first. Review avoidable rework, time lost awaiting decisions, late requirement defects, and user outcomes before adoption, and renegotiate on that evidence.

What Rev B changes from each source, stated openly so the negotiation is about these deltas and nothing else.

Amendments to the Rev 3 counter-draft
  • ΔBusiness decision owner replaced by a narrow executive sponsor. Authority reduced to release authorization, bounded risk acceptance, and escalation tie-breaks.Rationale: the org's history with standing product owners; the legitimate need (someone must accept business risk) is met without recreating the role.
  • ΔPrioritization returned to UX as A/R, evidence-based with published criteria; the sponsor ratifies the criteria and their application quarterly, not line items.Rationale: continuous content decisions follow the evidence; episodic gate decisions go up.
  • ΔRM removed from Responsible on all decision rows (tradeoffs, scope authorization, remediation priority, release, risk acceptance). RM retains A/R on every record, register, and process row, and gains the risk-acceptance record row explicitly.Rationale: aligns the RACI to Rev 3's own prose, which already states RM stewardship creates no product authority.
  • ΔUser comms / support removed as a RACI column; retained as a named function assigned to an existing communications or change-management lead, per Rev 3's own assignment rule.Rationale: keeps the table auditable; the function's working agreement stands unchanged.
